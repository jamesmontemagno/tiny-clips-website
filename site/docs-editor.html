Screenshot editor

Mark up a screenshot so it explains itself, then copy or save it.

Open the editor

The editor opens after a screenshot when it's turned on in Settings. To change this on Windows, use Open editor after capture in Settings → Screenshot. You can also reopen a screenshot from Recent Captures or the Clips Library, and open PNG, JPG, and WebP files with Tiny Clips.

Annotate

The toolbar has move, crop, rectangle, ellipse, arrow, line, freehand draw, text, numbered badge, emoji, and redact tools.

  • Arrows can be straight or curved.
  • Drag handles to move or resize an annotation after you place it.
  • Undo steps back through every change.
  • Zoom from 25% to 400%, and pan around while zoomed in.

Redact sensitive content

Use the Redact tool to hide part of the image. It offers blur, pixelate, and solid styles.

Use solid for passwords, keys, and other secrets. Blur and pixelate are built from the image itself, so large text or a light setting can stay readable.

Find sensitive text

Find sensitive text is in the Redact tool's side panel. It uses OCR on your device to suggest likely email addresses, phone numbers, and IP addresses.

  1. Run Find sensitive text. This alone doesn't change your image.
  2. Review each highlight, then approve or dismiss it.
  3. Apply the approved suggestions as opaque black redactions. You can undo them, and they're included when you copy or save.

It can miss things, so always review the whole image before you share it.

Add a background

Place your screenshot on a gradient background, then adjust the padding, rounded corners, and shadow.

On Windows, you can also use a local PNG, JPEG, or WebP image, with centered Fill or Fit placement. The image stays in the current editor only, and Remove or Reset clears it. Animated images and images above 32 megapixels aren't supported.

Export frames

Choose a frame to fit your screenshot to a set shape: Original, 1:1, 4:3, 16:9, 3:4, or 9:16. Then align the screenshot inside the frame with the 3×3 alignment control.
